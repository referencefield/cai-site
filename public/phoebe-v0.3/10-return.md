# Chapter 10 - Return

The house announced trouble before anyone told me what it was.

The shop-door was locked.

That was wrong enough to stop me.

The white curtain had been drawn across the glass. I tried the main door beneath the arched window and found that fastened too.

I knocked.

The sound traveled inward and came back empty.

I had been gone only a short while, yet the house looked as though it had withdrawn from the street.

I went around to the garden.

The chickens came toward me at once, absurdly eager, and for one instant their welcome made everything ordinary.

Then I saw the garden.

The storm had disordered it. Weeds had taken advantage of my absence. The arbor stood empty. Under its table lay a side-comb I recognized as mine, still where I must have dropped it before leaving.

No one had picked it up.

That frightened me more than the locked doors.

I tried the garden entrance.

Locked.

I knocked.

The door opened only enough for me to slip sideways through, exactly as Hepzibah sometimes opened it when she did not wish people in the street to see inside.

I entered without hesitation.

The door shut behind me.

The passage was dark after the garden.

A hand took mine.

Warm. Firm.

My heart knew Holgrave before my eyes did.

The relief was so immediate that I almost laughed.

Then I saw his face.

"What has happened?"

He drew me away from the parlor and into a large room I rarely used.

"Where are Hepzibah and Clifford?"

"Gone."

"Gone where?"

"I don't know."

The answer struck me as impossible.

Holgrave always seemed to know something, even if what he knew was only an opinion he had dressed too confidently.

Now he looked genuinely lost.

"Why is the house closed?"

He did not answer quickly enough.

I tried to move toward the parlor.

He stopped me.

"Phoebe."

"What?"

He said I would need strength.

I told him I had none and demanded the truth anyway.

Then he gave me a daguerreotype.

I recognized the Judge immediately.

The same face Holgrave had shown me before, with its hard lines made stronger by the plate.

"Why are you showing me this?"

He held out another.

The Judge again.

But not the same.

The face had no life in it.

I understood before Holgrave spoke.

"He is dead."

Holgrave told me the Judge sat in the next room.

He told me Hepzibah and Clifford had disappeared before he discovered the body. He had heard they were seen leaving in the storm. He had made the second picture because it might become evidence and because, he said, there were old reasons binding his own family strangely to the Judge's fate.

I barely heard that last part.

"Why have you not opened the doors?"

Holgrave began speaking of Clifford.

Of how terrible the flight might look.

Of how the Judge's death resembled the earlier death for which Clifford had suffered.

Of the hereditary physical tendency in the Pyncheon family.

Of his long belief that the circumstances around the old death had been arranged so that Clifford appeared guilty.

I listened until I understood the central point.

Clifford had not killed the Judge.

Holgrave believed the Judge himself had helped create the appearance of Clifford's guilt decades before.

The present death, occurring without Clifford there to be blamed for it, might finally expose the old mistake.

Except Clifford had run.

"Then we must not hide this," I said.

Holgrave hesitated.

"Phoebe—"

"Open the doors."

"We have to think what is best for them."

"Keeping a dead man hidden in the parlor cannot become better by another hour."

He looked at me.

I was trembling badly enough that I could feel it in my hands.

I did not feel strong.

I felt frightened and impatient and desperate for ordinary rules.

That was enough.

"Clifford is innocent," I said. "Then let the truth come in with everybody else."

Holgrave nodded slowly.

"You are right."

I moved toward the door.

He did not.

"Why are we still standing here?"

He looked at me with an expression I had never seen on him before.

Not argument.

Not theory.

Something much more dangerous because I understood it less.

"Phoebe," he said, "wait."

Behind the wall, Judge Pyncheon sat dead.

Hepzibah and Clifford were somewhere unknown.

The house needed to be opened.

And still I waited.
