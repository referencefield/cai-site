# Chapter 5 - The Window

Clifford did not speak to me the next morning.

He spoke near me.

He asked Hepzibah for coffee. He complained that the room was cold. He asked whether the organ-grinder would return to the street. When I passed him bread, he took it without looking at me.

I decided this was childish.

Then I remembered that I had frightened him because I wanted to prove to Hepzibah that I could make my own decisions.

I became very interested in buttering my toast.

After breakfast I went to the shop.

The little boy who had stared through the window on my first day came in for gingerbread. His name was Ned Higgins. He knew exactly how much gingerbread a penny should buy and watched my hand while I cut it.

"More," he said.

"That is a penny's worth."

"Miss Hepzibah gives more."

"Then Miss Hepzibah is ruining the business."

He considered this.

"She scowls, though."

"That costs nothing."

He laughed and ran out.

I saw Clifford in the doorway behind me.

I did not know how long he had been there.

"He likes you," he said.

"He likes gingerbread."

Clifford came into the shop.

I had never seen him there.

He looked around slowly at the jars and boxes.

"Hepzibah sells these?"

"Yes."

He touched a spool of thread.

"Why?"

I thought he was joking.

He was not.

"For money."

His face changed.

I wished I had answered differently.

He went to the window.

Outside, people were gathering along the street.

There was to be a procession that day. I had heard customers discussing it all morning. Music could already be heard several streets away.

Clifford leaned toward the glass.

"What is it?"

"A procession."

"I want to see."

"You can see from here."

"No."

He pointed upward.

"The window."

I knew which one he meant. An arched window above the street opened wide enough to give a better view.

"Perhaps from the room upstairs."

He had already turned.

I followed him.

On the stairs I said, "Wait for Hepzibah."

He continued.

"Clifford."

"I am not ill."

I almost said yes, you are.

Instead I said, "The stairs are steep."

He laughed.

It was not a pleasant laugh.

"There are many steep things in the world, Phoebe."

I let him go ahead.

At the window, the street had filled with people. Flags moved above hats. Children ran alongside adults. The music grew louder.

Clifford changed.

His hands gripped the sill, but his face was awake in a way I had rarely seen. He watched everything. He laughed when a horse resisted its rider. He called my attention to a banner. He leaned farther out.

"Careful."

He did not hear me.

I put my hand on his sleeve.

He pulled away.

"Don't."

"You're too far out."

"I can see."

"You can see from here."

"No."

He leaned again.

Below us the crowd moved as one body. For a moment Clifford seemed to move with it. I saw his weight shift.

I grabbed him around the arm.

He turned on me.

"Let go!"

I did not.

"Phoebe!"

He struck my hand away.

Not hard enough to hurt badly. Hard enough to shock me.

Then he put one foot against the lower frame as if he meant to climb farther into the opening.

I caught his coat.

"Stop!"

The music covered my voice.

Hepzibah came into the room.

She made a sound I had never heard from her.

She seized Clifford from behind.

Between us we pulled him back.

He fought us.

For several seconds he truly fought us.

Then the strength went out of him.

He collapsed into the chair.

Hepzibah knelt beside him.

I stood with my hands shaking.

Clifford looked at me.

"You always stop me."

I stared.

"You nearly fell."

"You don't know that."

"I saw you."

"You saw what you expected."

Hepzibah said his name.

He turned away.

I left the room.

In the kitchen I found a red mark across the back of my hand where he had struck it.

I was angry.

I was frightened.

I was ashamed of being angry because he was ill, then angry at myself for using illness to erase what he had done.

I put my hand under cold water.

Holgrave came in through the garden door.

"What happened?"

"Nothing."

He looked at my hand.

"That looks like nothing."

"Clifford hit me."

Holgrave's face changed.

"He what?"

"He didn't mean to hurt me."

"Did he hit you?"

"Yes."

"Then don't answer a different question."

I turned off the water.

"He wanted to see the procession."

I told him what happened.

Holgrave listened without interrupting.

When I finished, he said, "Was he going to jump?"

"I don't know."

"Did he say he was?"

"No."

"Did he say he wasn't?"

"No."

"Then you don't know."

"I wasn't going to wait until he was halfway out the window to ask."

"I didn't say you should have."

I looked at him.

"You sound as though I did something wrong."

"I don't know if you did."

"That's helpful."

"You want me to tell you that saving someone gives you the right to decide what they meant."

"I want you to stop talking."

He did.

That made me angrier.

I went upstairs.

Clifford's door was closed.

I raised my hand to knock.

The red mark had darkened.

I lowered it.

From inside the room I heard Hepzibah speaking softly.

Then Clifford said, very clearly, "I only wanted to see."

I went back downstairs before I heard her answer.
